Welcome Back, Creator

The Hub for Young Next-gen Knowledge

In Beginners Basic 1, you learned to speak the language of Scratch — sequencing, loops, variables, conditionals — and you discovered that Vedic Maths and coding are really the same kind of thinking wearing different clothes. You met sutras like Ekadhikena Purvena and Nikhilam, and you built your very first sprites.

This book asks a bigger question: now that you can code — what will you create? A Young Creator doesn't just write scripts, they direct scenes. Over four chapters you will make numbers dance across the stage, package a sutra into a tool you can reuse forever, record your own voice teaching a brand-new sutra, and choreograph a whole cast of sprites that talk to one another. By the final page, you won't just know more Scratch — you'll have made something worth showing your family.

🌀

Our philosophy: Spiral Learning

Notice that this book doesn't rush to new sutras right away. Chapters 1 and 2 return to Ekadhikena Purvena — the squaring-numbers-ending-in-5 trick you already know — and look at it through a new lens each time: first as animation, then as a reusable tool. Just like a sutra reveals a new layer each time you revisit it, this workbook spirals back to old ideas before reaching for new ones. One brand new sutra, Yavadunam, is waiting for you in Chapter 3 — you've already heard its name once, back in Book 1!

🎬

Think like a director

Every chapter frames its Scratch concept as a film-making job: animator, prop-maker, sound recordist, stage manager.

🧵

One sutra, many outfits

🗣️

Teach it to learn it

🤝

Sprites that talk

CHAPTER 1 · SPRITES & ANIMATION
1.1 · Costumes, Coordinates & Motion

What Makes a Sprite Move?

★ Big Idea

An animation is just a fast sequence of slightly-different pictures. Flip a comic book fast enough and it moves. In Scratch, those pictures are called costumes — and switching between them quickly is exactly how your sprite comes alive.

👕

Costumes

Every sprite can have many costumes — like outfits. Switching costumes fast makes it look like it's moving.

🧭

Coordinates (x, y)

The Stage is a grid. The centre is (0, 0). Bigger x = further right. Bigger y = further up.

🎞️

The Animation Loop

Change costume, wait a tiny moment, repeat forever. Three blocks, endless motion.

🔵 Motion vs 🟣 Looks — two families, one performer

Blue Motion blocks move your sprite around the stage. Purple Looks blocks change how it appears. A great animation almost always mixes both.

The animation-loop pattern
when 🏳 clicked forever next costume wait 0.2 seconds move 3 steps
Scratchy says: "Remember me from Book 1? I'm putting on my dancing shoes — this chapter, I'm going to show you a sutra instead of just saying it!"
Remember — A sprite with only ONE costume can still move (Motion blocks), but it can never animate (Looks blocks + costume switching). Motion is travel; animation is performance.

Ekadhikena Purvena — the Remix

★ Big Idea · Squaring Numbers Ending in 5

Back in Book 1 you met Ekadhikena Purvena — "by one more than the previous." For any number ending in 5, say N5: multiply N × (N + 1), then stick 25 on the end. That's the whole trick — no long multiplication needed!

Turn the Sutra Into 3 Costumes

A sutra is already a sequence of steps. An animation is already a sequence of costumes. Line them up, step for step, and the maths performs itself.

Vedic · Step 1

Spot the digit, N

In 65², the digit before the 5 is N = 6. That's the only number we need to remember.

→
Scratch · Costume 1

"Spotlight" costume

Sprite walks in and holds up a big 6 prop. A spotlight circle grows around it. Nothing else on stage yet.

Vedic · Step 2

Multiply N × (N + 1)

6 × 7 = 42. "One more than the previous" — six's neighbour, seven — joins in.

→
Scratch · Costume 2

"Merge" costume

A second prop, 7, glides in next to the 6. They collide with a little "poof" and merge into a single 42 prop.

Vedic · Step 3

Attach 25

Stick 25 onto the end of 42. 65² = 4225. Every N5-square ends this way — always 25, no exceptions.

→
Scratch · Costume 3

"Take a bow" costume

A banner unrolls reading 4225, the sprite takes a bow, and confetti (a Looks graphic effect!) sparkles across the stage.

Stitching the 3 costumes into one script
when 🏳 clicked switch costume to spotlight wait 1 second switch costume to merge wait 1 second switch costume to take-a-bow say join front 25 for 2 seconds
Scratchy says: "Notice front is just a variable holding N×(N+1) — you calculate it once, then let three costumes perform it. That's the whole build on the next page!"

CHAPTER 2 · CUSTOM BLOCKS
2.1 · My Blocks & Inputs

Teach Your Computer a Trick — Once

★ Big Idea

Imagine teaching your computer a special trick one time. After that, you can ask it to perform the trick again and again — on any number you like — just by asking. That's a custom block: code you write once and reuse forever.

💗

My Blocks

The pink category at the bottom of the palette. Click "Make a Block" and you invent a brand-new one, from scratch.

🈳

Inputs

A blank space in your trick that changes every time you use it — like a Mad Libs blank, but for numbers.

♻️

Reusability

Write the logic once, inside the block's definition. Call it as many times as you want, anywhere in your project.

Why bother building a block at all?

😩

Without a custom block

To square 15², 45² and 95², you'd paste the same 4 maths blocks three separate times — 12 blocks total. Find a mistake? Fix it in three different places.

🤩

With a custom block

Write the 4 maths blocks once, inside a definition. Then just call square ender 5 (15), (45), (95). One mistake, one fix.

Scratchy says: "I taught myself the Ekadhikena Purvena trick back in Chapter 1. I'm not doing it the long way ever again — this chapter, I build a machine that remembers it for me."
Remember — My Blocks is the only pink category where you decide what goes in it. Every other coloured family came ready-made; this one is yours to invent.

A Sutra Is Already a Custom Block

A sutra is a rule you state once and apply to endless numbers. A custom block is a script you write once and call with endless inputs. They're the same idea, wearing different clothes.

Vedic · Step 1

State the rule once

"Ekadhikena Purvena: N × (N+1), then 25." Written a single time — in one sentence, forever.

→
Scratch · Step 1

Define the block once

Inside define square ender 5 (num), the logic is written a single time — inside one script, forever.

Vedic · Step 2

Plug in any N5 number

65, 15, 195 — the rule never changes. Only the number sliding into it changes.

→
Scratch · Step 2

Fill in the input

square ender 5 (65) — the script never changes. Only the value sliding into num changes.

Vedic · Step 3

Get a fresh answer, every time

The same short rule quietly handles every N5 number that will ever exist.

→
Scratch · Step 3

The block replies, every time

The same short script quietly handles every call you'll ever make to it.

Defining once, calling many times
define square ender 5 (num) set N to (letter 1 of num) set front to (N × (N+1)) say (join front "25") for 1.5 seconds

CHAPTER 3 · NEW SUTRA
3.2 · Meet a Brand-New Sutra

Yavadunam — Squaring Near 100

★ Big Idea · "Whatever the Deficiency…"

Yavadunam Tavadunikritya Vargam Cha Yojayet — "whatever the deficiency, lessen it further by that much, and set up the square of the deficiency." You actually heard this sutra's name once already, back in Book 1's Conditionals chapter — this time, you'll learn the full trick.

It squares any number close to 100 in three steps — no long multiplication needed.

1️⃣

Find the deficiency, D

D = 100 − N. How far below 100 does your number sit?

2️⃣

Subtract D again

N − D gives the left part of your answer.

3️⃣

Square D, attach it

D² (as 2 digits) becomes the right part of your answer.

WORKED EXAMPLES
98²D = 100−98 = 2 → left = 98−2 = 96 → right = 2² = 04 → 9604
97²D = 3 → left = 97−3 = 94 → right = 3² = 09 → 9409
94²D = 6 → left = 94−6 = 88 → right = 6² = 36 → 8836
Above 100 works too — for a number like 104, find the surplus instead: S = 104−100 = 4. Left = 104+4 = 108. Right = 4² = 16. Answer: 10816. Same idea, opposite direction!

CHAPTER 4 · BROADCASTING
4.1 · Events & Messages

Make Your Sprites Talk

★ Big Idea

Imagine one player on a team shouting "Go!" — everyone else instantly knows what to do next, without being told individually. That's a broadcast: one sprite sends a message, and every sprite listening can react.

📣

broadcast

Sends a named message to every sprite in the project, instantly. Doesn't wait for a reply.

✋

broadcast and wait

Same message — but pauses the sender until every listener has finished reacting.

👂

when I receive

A hat block that wakes up the instant its named message arrives — this sprite's turn to act.

Two sprites, one conversation
when 🏳 clicked say "97 is 3 away from 100!" for 2 seconds broadcast subtract-again
when I receive subtract-again say "97 minus 3 is 94!" for 2 seconds
🧠 Think Like a Programmer

Notice the second sprite never asks "has the first sprite finished?" — it just waits, silently, until its message arrives. This is exactly how a relay runner waits for the baton: not by watching the clock, but by waiting for the hand-off.
